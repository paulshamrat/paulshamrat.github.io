This two-week research digest brings exciting developments for the CDKL5 Deficiency Disorder (CDD) community, highlighting breakthroughs in understanding the CDKL5 protein, new tools for research, and crucial insights into diagnosis.

A significant discovery sheds light on the fundamental behavior of the CDKL5 protein. Researchers found that the CDKL5 kinase undergoes a process called liquid-liquid phase separation (LLPS), forming temporary, membraneless compartments within cells, critical for its function [Boggio Bozzo S et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41807047/). Importantly, common CDD-causing mutations that truncate the protein’s C-terminal region impair this LLPS process and reduce the protein’s activity. This finding provides a deeper understanding of how CDD develops at a molecular level, potentially opening new avenues for therapeutic intervention.

Advancing our ability to study CDD, a new open-source platform was developed for high-throughput calcium imaging and all-optical physiology in human stem cell-derived neurons [Afshar-Saber W et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41801223/). This powerful tool allows scientists to investigate neuronal activity at single-cell and network levels, and has already been successfully applied to model CDKL5 Deficiency. Such platforms are vital for functional phenotyping, understanding disease mechanisms, and accelerating the search for new treatments.

Finally, a retrospective study underscored the importance of comprehensive diagnostic reassessment, particularly as individuals transition from childhood to adult care for epilepsy [Fukuyama T et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41804683/). This research found that nearly one in five patients had their epilepsy diagnosis revised, with genetic etiologies like CDKL5 deficiency being newly identified in some cases. This highlights the ongoing need for thorough diagnostic evaluation to ensure accurate diagnoses and optimal management for those with complex epilepsies, including CDD.

These recent findings offer a clearer picture of CDKL5’s role, provide innovative research tools, and reinforce the importance of precise diagnosis, collectively moving the field forward towards better understanding and treatment of CDD.
